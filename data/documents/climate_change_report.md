# Climate Change: Causes, Effects, and Solutions

## What is Climate Change?

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While some climate change occurs naturally, since the mid-20th century human activities have been the primary driver of accelerating changes in Earth's climate system.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has concluded with over 95% scientific consensus that current climate change is primarily caused by human activities, particularly the burning of fossil fuels.

## The Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface. When the Sun's energy reaches the Earth, some is reflected back to space and some is absorbed. The absorbed energy warms the Earth and is re-emitted as infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the atmosphere trap some of this heat, keeping the planet warm enough to support life.

Key greenhouse gases include:
- **Carbon dioxide (CO2)**: Released by burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes
- **Methane (CH4)**: Released by livestock digestion, rice paddies, landfills, and natural gas leaks
- **Nitrous oxide (N2O)**: Released from agricultural fertilizers and industrial processes
- **Water vapor**: The most abundant greenhouse gas, amplified by warming
- **Fluorinated gases**: Industrial chemicals used in refrigeration and manufacturing

## Human Causes of Climate Change

### Fossil Fuels
Burning coal, oil, and natural gas for electricity, heat, and transportation is the largest single source of greenhouse gas emissions globally, accounting for roughly 75% of global emissions. Carbon dioxide released from combustion has increased atmospheric CO2 from 280 ppm (pre-industrial) to over 420 ppm today.

### Deforestation
Forests absorb CO2 from the atmosphere. When forests are cleared for agriculture or development, stored carbon is released. Deforestation accounts for approximately 10-15% of global emissions.

### Agriculture
Agricultural practices including livestock farming (methane from cattle), rice cultivation, and synthetic fertilizers (nitrous oxide) contribute about 10% of global emissions.

### Industrial Processes
Cement production, steel manufacturing, and chemical processes release significant amounts of CO2 and other greenhouse gases.

### Waste
Decomposing organic waste in landfills produces methane. Wastewater treatment also releases greenhouse gases.

## Effects of Climate Change

### Rising Temperatures
Global average temperatures have risen approximately 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels. The IPCC warns that exceeding 1.5°C will trigger significantly worse impacts, and exceeding 2°C could be catastrophic.

### Melting Ice and Rising Seas
Arctic sea ice is declining at approximately 13% per decade. The Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets are losing mass. Global sea levels have risen by about 20 cm since 1900 and are accelerating. By 2100, sea levels could rise by 0.3 to 1 meter or more, threatening coastal cities and small island nations.

### Extreme Weather Events
Climate change is increasing the frequency and intensity of:
- Hurricanes and typhoons
- Droughts and heatwaves
- Heavy rainfall and flooding
- Wildfires

### Ocean Changes
Oceans absorb about 30% of CO2 emissions, causing ocean acidification that threatens coral reefs and marine ecosystems. Oceans also absorb over 90% of the excess heat from global warming.

### Ecosystem Disruption
Species are shifting their habitats poleward and to higher elevations. Migration patterns are changing. Coral bleaching events are becoming more frequent. Up to one million species are at risk of extinction if warming continues unchecked.

### Human Health Impacts
Heatwaves cause heat stress and death. Expanding ranges of mosquitoes spread diseases like malaria and dengue fever. Air pollution from wildfires affects respiratory health. Food insecurity increases as agricultural yields decline in some regions.

### Economic Impacts
The economic costs of climate change include damage from extreme weather, agricultural losses, healthcare costs, and the cost of adapting infrastructure. The Global Commission on the Economy and Climate estimates climate change could cost the global economy $23 trillion per year by 2100 under a high-emissions scenario.

## Solutions to Climate Change

### Transitioning to Renewable Energy
Solar, wind, hydroelectric, and geothermal energy produce electricity without greenhouse gas emissions. The cost of solar and wind power has fallen dramatically — over 90% since 2010 — making renewables now the cheapest source of new electricity generation in most of the world.

### Energy Efficiency
Improving efficiency in buildings, transportation, and industry reduces energy demand. LED lighting, better insulation, and electric heat pumps are cost-effective measures.

### Electrifying Transportation
Replacing gasoline and diesel vehicles with electric vehicles (EVs) reduces transport emissions when powered by clean electricity. EV adoption is accelerating globally.

### Carbon Capture
Technologies that capture CO2 from the atmosphere or industrial sources can help offset emissions. Natural solutions include reforestation and restoring wetlands and mangroves.

### Sustainable Agriculture
Regenerative farming practices can reduce emissions and even sequester carbon in soils. Reducing food waste also lowers emissions significantly.

### Policy and International Agreements
The Paris Agreement (2015) commits countries to limiting warming to well below 2°C. Carbon pricing through taxes or cap-and-trade systems creates economic incentives to reduce emissions. Government regulations and subsidies can accelerate the transition to clean energy.

### Individual Actions
While systemic change is most critical, individual actions also matter:
- Reduce air travel and use public transport or EVs
- Switch to plant-rich diets
- Reduce energy use at home
- Choose renewable electricity providers
- Support climate-conscious businesses and politicians

## The Path Forward

The window to limit warming to 1.5°C is rapidly closing. Global emissions must reach net zero by around 2050 to have a reasonable chance of meeting this target. This requires an unprecedented transformation of the global energy system, economy, and land use — but it is technically and economically feasible.

The costs of acting on climate change are significantly lower than the costs of inaction. Investments in clean energy and efficiency create jobs, improve air quality, and enhance energy security while reducing climate risk.

Climate change is the defining challenge of this century. The choices made in the next decade will shape the world for generations to come.
